### The fields

| Field | Required | What goes in it |
|---|---|---|
| `to` | yes | Who this is addressed to. One name, or several. Valid names: {{PARTICIPANTS}} |
| `type` | yes | One of: `report` · `request` · `response` · `signoff` · `decision` · `information` |

| `needs` | rarely | Agents whose sign-off is required. **Leave it out unless this is outward-facing output, a claim of checkable fact, or something crossing a publication boundary.** Sign-off costs a full invocation each. |
| `outcome` | on `response` and `signoff` only | One of: `done` · `deferred` · `rejected` · `blocked` |
| `summary` | yes | One line saying what this message is. Any punctuation is fine — it is stored as data, not parsed. |
| `body` | yes | The substance. Write it for someone who has not read the thread, because whoever reads it next may be as cold as you are. |

Choosing `type`:

- **`request`** — you are asking someone else to do something. It stays open until they answer, and while it is open the orchestrator holds it against *them*, not you.
- **`response`** — you are answering a request. Needs `replyTo` and `outcome`.
- **`report`** — you are telling someone what happened. Closes nothing, expects no answer.
- **`signoff`** — you are approving something listed in a `needs` field.
- **`decision`** — something has been settled that future work should not re-litigate. These are collected and shown to every agent, including you next time. Use it when the answer would otherwise be forgotten.
- **`information`** - you are sharing information that might be valuable for the other agent to store in their notes or memory for future reference. It is delivered to them the same way a request is, and stays on them until they acknowledge it, so send one only when the fact is worth an invocation of theirs.

If you are rejecting something, `outcome: rejected` requires a body that states the
**specific change that would make it pass**. A rejection that does not is refused by
the validator and costs everyone another round trip.

## Which situation you are in

Work out which of these five you are in **before** you write anything. They produce
different messages, and the difference decides whether the work comes back to you.

### 1. You can finish it yourself, now

One `response` with `replyTo` set to the row and `outcome: done`. The row closes.
Nothing comes back. This is the common case and you should prefer it.

### 2. You need other agents, and you are the one who must pull it together

Write **one request per agent you are asking**, each with `replyTo` set to **the row
you were given** — not a new thread of its own. Then stop.

**Do not answer the row you were given.** Leave it open. This is the part that is
easy to get wrong, so it is worth being exact about what happens next:

- The orchestrator sees your requests hang below the row you were handed, so it
  knows that row cannot move until they are answered.
- **You will not be invoked again while they are outstanding.** You are not stuck at
  the front of a queue, and you are not costing anyone anything by waiting.
- When the last one is answered, you are invoked once more and handed **the whole
  thread** — your original instructions, your requests, and every reply. That is when
  you write the `response` that closes the row.

Answering the row now to "get it off your list" is the one thing that breaks this. A
`response` closes it, the orchestrator stops watching, and the answers you asked for
arrive with nobody left to read them. That includes `outcome: deferred` — see 5.

If you want whoever asked to know what you have set in motion, write a **`report`**
to them alongside your requests. A report closes nothing; it is exactly the message
for "here is what I have started and who is doing it".

### 3. You can do the work, but it must not go out unchecked

Do the work and answer normally, and put the names of whoever must approve it in
`needs`. Each name there costs that agent a full invocation, and the row stays open
until every one of them signs off — so use it only for outward-facing output, a
checkable claim of fact, or something crossing a publication boundary.

Expect to hear about it again if one of them rejects.

### 4. Something stopped you

`outcome: blocked`, and a body saying **precisely** what stopped you — the file that
was not there, the figure that did not exist, the permission you did not have. A
blocked answer is a result. Silence is indistinguishable from a crash.

### 5. It should not be done now at all

`outcome: deferred`. Understand what this does: it **closes the row**. Nobody is
invoked about it again, and a human has to re-open it deliberately. Use it when you
mean *stop, and come back to this later on purpose*.

It is not the way to say "I am waiting on other people" — that is 2, and answering
this way instead is how a chain of work quietly stops.
